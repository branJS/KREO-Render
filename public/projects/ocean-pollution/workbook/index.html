01 / IntroductionThe Brief
002 / 042

The brief.

The brief asks us to create a digital campaign for a South West organisation or community group connected to water, the coast, or marine environments.

The campaign must communicate one clear, focused message through a sequence of key frames and a short motion piece designed for social media. The final work needs to be screen-first, clear, visually consistent, and suitable for fast mobile viewing.

Caption

The project focuses on communication, awareness, and behaviour change through digital design.

Microplastic pollution as an invisible, accumulating environmental and human health concern.

Rather than showing plastic pollution only as visible waste in the ocean, this campaign focuses on the invisible movement of microplastic particles.

03 / IssueWhy This Issue Matters
011 / 042

Why this issue matters.

Microplastics are often too small to see, which makes the problem feel distant or abstract.

However, microplastics are not only present in the ocean. They can come from textiles, tyres, wastewater, sewage systems, road runoff, and the breakdown of larger plastic waste.

This makes the issue relevant to everyday life, especially in coastal cities like Plymouth.

03 / IssueKey Facts
012 / 042

Key facts.

Key facts informing the campaign, each shaping a specific decision in the visual or written direction.

  • F·01Microplastics are plastic fragments smaller than 5mm (Thompson et al., 2004).
  • F·02They can come from textiles, tyres, single-use plastics, and degraded plastic waste (Thompson et al., 2004; Browne et al., 2011).
  • F·03A single domestic wash can release microfibres into wastewater systems (Browne et al., 2011).
  • F·04Large amounts of plastic waste enter the ocean from land-based sources each year (Jambeck et al., 2015).
  • F·05Marine litter is strongly connected to human behaviour, consumption, and waste systems (GESAMP, 2016).

These facts helped shape the campaign around movement: land -> water -> ocean -> air -> human body. See p. 42 for full references.

03 / IssueResearch Insight

Research insight

Pollution in the ocean does not stay in the ocean.

People often imagine pollution as something "out there" in the sea. This campaign challenges that assumption by showing microplastic pollution moving through water, air, and human space (Dris et al., 2016; Allen et al., 2019). This became the foundation for the campaign message.

07 / ProductionMotion Workflow, Experimentation
037 / 042

Motion workflow: first-to-last frame.

Rather than generating motion from a single image, I experimented with a first-to-last frame (FLF2V) workflow in ComfyUI, feeding the first frame of one keyframe and the last frame of the next as temporal anchors. This forces the model to interpolate a coherent motion path between two specific visual states, rather than generating arbitrary movement from one image alone.

Test · 01 Complete
Frame 1 -> 2
Ocean surface -> fibres

Cross-frame motion reads cleanly. The FLF2V constraint produced a natural visual bridge, the ocean surface dissolves into the fibre close-up without abrupt cuts.

Test · 02 In Progress
Frame 3 -> 4
Tilt-shift -> breath

Transition between the tilt-shift pollution frame and the breath close-up. Temporal anchoring under active refinement, workflow parameters being adjusted to improve motion coherence.

Both clips generated via ComfyUI Wan 2.2 VACE, FLF2V (first-to-last frame) workflow. Additional test clips will be added to this folder as experimentation continues.

Concept explanation.
